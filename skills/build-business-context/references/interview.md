# The interview

Read this after the research has been reported back and corrected. Research can say what the business *is*. It cannot say what the business *wants*, where it is losing money, or how it ships code. The interview is the single most important input for keeping future recommendations grounded.

## How to ask

- **Small, themed batches, not one giant questionnaire.** Three or four related questions at a time; then react to the answers and ask smart follow-ups before moving to the next theme. This feels like a conversation, surfaces better answers, and lets you adapt: if they say "we have no engineers", the deployment follow-ups change entirely.
- **Pull from the question bank below; don't ask every question mechanically.** Skip questions that research or transcripts already answered, or just confirm them. Skip ones that don't apply.
- **Make questions clickable wherever a short menu of answers fits.** They are far faster to answer than a wall of text. One clickable question per decision, with 2–5 options.
  - Each `→ options:` line below gives a starting menu. Fill it with what the research found (the platform detected, their real channels, the competitors found) and lead with the answer the research most supports, labelled "(likely)". Accurate options make a menu usable; generic ones don't.
  - Where a menu below is longer than five, offer the five the research supports most. The user can always type a different answer.
  - Where more than one answer can apply, say so in the question.
- **Keep the open questions as prose**, asked as a short follow-up after a clickable batch. Two kinds resist a menu: exact numbers (target and current ROAS, conversion rate, AOV), and the "what about your store makes standard metrics misleading?" narrative. That one's whole value is the unprompted answer; never flatten it into options. They are marked `→ prose` below.
- **Keep it human.** If an answer opens a door ("paid is killing us"), walk through it before moving on. You don't need an answer to every question: capture what they have, mark the rest, and move on. A shorter honest file beats a padded one full of guesses.

Cover the themes roughly in this order.

## 1. Goals for the year

- What does success look like this year for the business?
- If you could only improve ONE of these this year, which? (Single choice. This is the priority signal that feeds **Goals & priorities** in the file, distinct from what they merely track in theme 3.)
  - `→ options:` Conversion rate / AOV / ROAS / Total revenue / Retention. Adapt to the business: add wholesale growth, a sub-brand or a new market if research surfaced one.
- Any specific initiative or launch that has to land this year?
- *Follow-ups:* Revenue or growth target? New market or channel? A turnaround on something that's underperforming?

## 2. Where you think you're losing money

This tells future analysis where to look first.

- Where on the site does it feel like money leaks out?
  - `→ options (more than one may apply):` Checkout / Cart / Product pages / Search / Collection pages / Mobile against desktop / Paid traffic that doesn't convert. Leaks rarely have one home.
- Have you seen this in data, or is it a gut feel?
  - `→ options:` Seen it in data / Gut feel / Both. (Label which in the file.)
- *Follow-ups:* Any specific product pages, collections or flows you already worry about? Does checkout feel solid, or do people fall out there? How is mobile against desktop?

## 3. Performance targets and current numbers

Numbers the user gives are far more trustworthy than anything inferred.

- `→ prose:` What's your target against current ROAS? Target against current conversion rate? AOV? These are exact figures; ask in plain text.
- Which of these do you already track week to week? (Feeds the **Performance targets** table: the same metrics as theme 1, but here the lens is what they monitor, not their one priority.)
  - `→ options (more than one may apply):` Conversion rate / AOV / ROAS / Revenue / Sessions or traffic / Bounce or exit rate / Retention or repeat rate.
- *Follow-ups:* A benchmark you measure yourself against?

## 4. The customer, in their words

Capture these gotchas carefully; they are exactly what prevents wrong analysis.

- Is there a B2B / B2C split? Roughly what's the revenue mix?
  - `→ options:` Pure B2C / Pure B2B / Mostly B2C with some B2B / Mostly B2B with some B2C / Even mix.
- Do you have buyer personas, formal or informal?
  - `→ options:` Formal documented personas / Informal sense of them / None yet.
- `→ prose:` Here's the important one: is there anything about how your store works that makes standard e-commerce metrics misleading? (Minimum order values or quantities, multi-visit research before buying, bulk or trade buyers, a page that "converts badly" but is doing its job.) **Keep this open.** The unprompted answer is the whole point.
- *Follow-ups:* Who's your most valuable customer? How do you tell a high-value customer from a tire-kicker in your data? Any past analysis that got your business wrong?

## 5. Tech stack and site quirks

- What platform is the store on? (Confirm what research found.)
  - `→ options:` Lead with the platform research detected, labelled "(likely)"; then Shopify / BigCommerce / Magento or Adobe Commerce / Salesforce Commerce / WooCommerce / Custom.
- Is checkout the platform's native checkout, or customized?
  - `→ options:` Native / Customized / Fully custom / Not sure.
- Custom theme, or a stock one?
  - `→ options:` Custom theme / Stock theme / Lightly customized stock.
- *Follow-ups:* Who's your payment processor, and any separate vaulting provider? Key apps: reviews, search, loyalty, subscriptions, analytics? Anything fragile where a code change is risky? Is the code repository connected? Any staging store?

## 6. Deployment and dev team

This decides what kinds of recommendations are even realistic.

- Who builds and ships changes right now?
  - `→ options:` Internal engineers / An agency / A freelancer / No one technical. (This answer reshapes the follow-ups; branch on it.)
- Is there a staging or test environment, or do changes go straight to production?
  - `→ options:` Staging then production / Straight to production / Not sure.
- How cautious is the team about merging changes, especially AI-suggested ones?
  - `→ options:` Very cautious, heavy review / Moderate, normal review / Move fast, light review.
- *Follow-ups:* What's the typical process to get a change live? Who reviews and approves? For an agency: retainer or hourly, how fast, new to AI workflows? What kind of change is easiest and hardest to get merged?

## 7. Support and customer service workflow

- How big is your support team?
  - `→ options:` No dedicated support / 1–2 people / 3–10 / 10+.
- Do you use a ticketing system, or handle it some other way?
  - `→ options:` Dedicated tool (Zendesk, Gorgias and so on) / Shared inbox / Ad hoc, no system.
- *Follow-ups:* When something breaks on the site, how does it get flagged and fixed? Cross-team visibility, or does it live in inboxes? Any appetite for process change?

## 8. Marketing and channels

- Which channels do you use today?
  - `→ options (more than one may apply):` Paid search / Paid social / Email / SMS / Organic social / SEO / Affiliate or influencer / Marketplaces. Lead with the ones research surfaced.
- Which feels like your single biggest near-term growth opportunity?
  - `→ options:` The same channel list as a single choice, framed as "most upside".
- *Follow-ups:* Which channels are working and which are underused? Email program maturity? Paid against organic mix? Strong seasonal patterns? Any channel you know you're neglecting?

## 9. How you want the analysis to work with you

This whole theme is option-shaped; ask it as one or two clickable batches.

- Direct and concise, or more thorough?
  - `→ options:` Direct and concise / Thorough and detailed / Depends on the task.
- Plain language, or comfortable with technical detail?
  - `→ options:` Plain language / Technical is fine / Mix depending on topic.
- When you ask for something, do you want it produced, or options to choose from?
  - `→ options:` Just produce it / Give me options first / Depends on the stakes.
- Should big tasks start with a plan?
  - `→ options:` Always plan first / Only for big or risky tasks / Just go.
- *Follow-ups:* How opinionated should it be? Any tone or formatting preferences? Brand spelling conventions to always apply?

## Closing

- Anything important about the business I didn't ask that should always be kept in mind?
- Of everything we covered, what are the two or three things that must never be got wrong about your store?

Use the answer to that last question to build the standing-reminders checklist at the end of the file.
